# Retro Facilitation Plan — NovaLend, Post Two Missed Sprints

**Why not a standard format:** Start/Stop/Continue or a generic Sailboat retro assumes a team with enough psychological safety to speak freely in the open. This team has just missed two sprints and morale is visibly low — the format below is built to surface honest input *before* anyone has to say it out loud in a room, and to make sure the retro produces action the team actually believes will happen, since a retro that generates promises nobody keeps is what erodes morale further.

## Format: "Anchors & Wind" with anonymous pre-work

### Stage 1 — Async pre-work (24h before the live session, opens for the remote contractor too)
Everyone, including the contractor, submits anonymously (shared doc or form, no names attached) under three prompts:
1. **Anchors** — what dragged the last two sprints down?
2. **Wind** — what actually worked, even a little?
3. **Unsaid** — one thing you need to say but haven't said out loud yet.

This does two jobs at once: it captures the contractor's input without forcing them into a rushed live window, and it gets the hardest things on the table before anyone has to own them publicly in front of the team.

### Stage 2 — Live session (60 min, held in the 2–4pm Lagos / 9–11am contractor overlap window)

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–5 min | Check-in: one word for how you're feeling, round-robin, SM goes first to model honesty ("frustrated, but hopeful"). |
| 5–15 min | SM reads out the anonymized pre-work themes and clusters them live on screen — no attribution, ever. |
| 15–40 min | Facilitated discussion, one theme at a time, round-robin speaking order (not open floor). Question per theme: **"What would need to be true for this not to happen a third time?"** |
| 40–55 min | Convert each theme into exactly one action: one owner, one date, logged live and visible to everyone before the call ends. |
| 55–60 min | Close: round-robin, one thing each person will personally do differently next sprint. |

### Handling a dominant voice
- Before the session: a private, non-confrontational heads-up — *"I'm going to time-box everyone today, including you — appreciate you letting others go first on a couple of these."*
- During the session: round-robin structure by default, not open discussion, so no one has to compete for airtime.
- If it happens anyway: redirect without a public callout — *"Let's hear from [name] on this one before we come back to you."*

### Drawing out silent teammates
- The anonymous pre-work already captures their input even if they say nothing live.
- Direct, low-pressure invitations work better than open ones: *"[Name], was there anything in the pre-work that didn't come up yet you'd want to add?"* — never *"does anyone have anything to add?"*, which silent teammates reliably don't answer.

### Sample discussion questions actually used
- "When we missed the last two sprints, was it clearer to you before or after the sprint that we were off track?"
- "Was there a moment you wanted to raise a blocker and didn't? What made that hard?"
- "If NovaWallet's API slips again this sprint, what do we do differently than last time?"

### Converting output into owned action
- No theme leaves the room without an owner and a date within the current sprint.
- If no one present can own it (e.g., the NovaWallet dependency itself), it's explicitly logged as "escalated to SM" rather than left hanging — the team should never leave a retro unsure whether something will actually happen.
- Actions are posted in the team channel immediately after the session and checked at the next standup and the next retro — visible follow-through matters more than the retro conversation itself for rebuilding trust.
